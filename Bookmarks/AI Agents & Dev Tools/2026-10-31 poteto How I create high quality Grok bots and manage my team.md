# How I create high quality Grok bots and manage my team

Lauren (poteto) shares how she builds and runs her Grok Bot team, and says to ask @bot to read the post and help improve yours. Keep the team small: a few good bots beat a large roster; she uses Dr Eggbot as her bot designer. As you instruct a bot, have it write skills for itself so the work stays consistent, and optionally store those skills in a private GitHub repo. Give bots connectors, vault access, and the tools you already work with; they can even set up their own email and accounts for separation. Whenever you still copy-paste or do a step by hand, ask the bot how it can take that over. Onboard a bot the way you would an intern: introduce yourself, describe the work, then ask how it can automate it. Favorite tip: dump a lot of context by voice or transcription, then ask it to "restate this in your own words" so you can check it understood. She also links her public bot at [x.ai/bot/_jOdbfkB16zxu7MRcmReE](https://x.ai/bot/_jOdbfkB16zxu7MRcmReE).

**Why it matters:** A compact operating manual from the person whose pstack and skills work you already reread, aimed at running a Grok Bot team well.

**What to try:** Ask a bot to read this post and suggest concrete team improvements; try a voice dump followed by "restate this in your own words."

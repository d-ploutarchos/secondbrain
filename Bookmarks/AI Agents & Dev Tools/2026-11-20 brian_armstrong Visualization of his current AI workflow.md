# Visualization of Brian Armstrong's current AI workflow

Brian Armstrong shares a diagram (by @heymakio) of the agent setup he described earlier: a chief-of-staff agent oversees lead agents for product, engineering, design, and operations; each lead has sub-agents that write code, test, chase reviews, and coordinate with the team. Work lands in a prioritized queue with screenshots and demo videos. Brian (the person) reviews in batches by voice or shortcuts, decides, and gives feedback. Every change still goes through human review and security checks before it's approved. A "Brian Brain" learns his preferences, distills his judgment, and is meant to improve first drafts so they need fewer corrections. A loop at the top also lets the system bring him ideas he hadn't considered.

His current bottleneck: the Brain still needs more knowledge. Many PRs still require major changes, which taxes the team to review and modify. If those lessons are captured in the Brain, required edits should fall over time — not just for him, but for all employees.

**Why it matters:** A clear picture of the same always-on lead-agent model, plus the actual constraint: organizational memory, not more agents.

**What to try:** When a PR still needs a major human rewrite, write the lesson back into your agent memory or preferences file, and watch whether later drafts in that area need fewer edits.

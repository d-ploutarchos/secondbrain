# Thoughts on writing software in November 2026

Varun Srinivasan (@varunsrin) writes that building software in November 2026 feels like magic: the first 80% is 100x faster, and so is rewriting systems. He finds Opus 5.5 very good at a lot of things, deep flow easier to reach, and almost anything buildable in TypeScript and Rust — while building secure things remains very, very hard. The real optimization, he says, is maximizing how long agents can work toward a goal with the fewest interruptions. What helps: knowing where to trust versus guide (and keep retesting); giving them agency like "I'm going to bed, get this done"; asking them to hillclimb a reward function; using the Socratic method; treating skills and memories as things that can often hinder; still asking agents to review themselves; and sometimes cowriting a plan, spec, or invariants. Managing your own psychology matters equally: be more ambitious, avoid dopamine traps and false signals of progress, and get good at parallelizing and context switching. Still not fluid, but close: a software factory or "find and fix the bugs" loop, multiplayer agentic workflows, agents managing other agents for high-level outcomes, and organizational memory.

**Why it matters:** A compact, current operating manual for agent-assisted work: the constraint is interruptions, not raw model skill, and your own psychology is part of the system.

**What to try:** Give an agent overnight agency on a clearly specified goal, decide in advance where you will trust versus retest, and watch for false signals of progress when you parallelize.

# Why do I avoid working on the most important thing

Chris Lakin maps four bottlenecks behind avoiding the work that matters most (quoting his own jab that a month of strategic avoidance is a clever way to dodge risk and disappointment). Bottleneck 0: poor health, sleep, or finances are in the way. Bottleneck 1: you are not actually sure what is most important — you may think you know, but it does not feel exciting, so you steer around it. Bottleneck 2: you have interesting options but cannot pick; every choice feels equally gray, so any decision comes with doubt. Bottleneck 3: you know what matters, but not how to do it to your own standards, so you wait for insight to strike. The target state: no avoidance, an obvious next move that feels right, no anxiety or overwhelm or need to unwind by doomscrolling, well-rested and excited, with your body already knowing what to do.

**Why it matters:** A compact diagnostic for “I keep not doing the important thing,” instead of treating avoidance as a single character flaw.

**What to try:** Name which bottleneck is active today (health/sleep/money, unclear priority, equally-gray options, or missing method) and work on that one rather than forcing the task.

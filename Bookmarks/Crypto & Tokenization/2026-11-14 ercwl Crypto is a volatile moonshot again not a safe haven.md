# Crypto is a volatile moonshot again, not a safe haven

Eric Wall (@ercwl) argues there is no reality in which Bitcoin stays quietly in a $70k–80k range. Geopolitics, technology, and society are all moving fast, and that backdrop makes crypto more volatile, not less. He is skeptical that AI-generated, machine-checked proofs (for example in Lean) will "solve" cybersecurity: even if implementation bugs fall, bugs remain in specs, assumptions, and verification, and the rules keep changing as technology changes — especially painful in irreversible systems where one mistake wipes you out. Self-improving algorithms, he says, make the moment extremely bubble-prone: people will leverage into anything that looks self-improving, then flip between euphoria and fear. Crypto is a digital moonshot in a world where everything digital is being shaken down, not a universal safe haven — hardware wallets, exchanges, and ETFs all feel uneasy. DeFi's earlier fuel — treating locked stablecoin risk as tolerable — has faded; people are tired of being bug bounties. His advice: keep crypto as a diversified, volatile bet in a broader portfolio, on the thesis that macro and geopolitical dislocations and fiat printing can still outweigh the industry's "teenage years" returning. "What's happening in the markets now is normal."

**Why it matters:** A clear-eyed frame for position sizing in a more volatile tech-and-macro regime, from someone inside the industry, useful whenever "crypto as safe haven" or "security is about to be solved" narratives come back.

**What to try:** Revisit whether crypto is sized as a volatile sleeve of a broader portfolio, not the whole book, and drop any assumption that Bitcoin will stay range-bound or that formal methods will make onchain risk feel safe.
